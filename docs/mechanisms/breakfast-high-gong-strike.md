# Mechanism: Breakfast High Gong Strike

## Status

Dream

## Purpose

Transform the breakfast route signal into a bright high-pitched gong strike.

This dossier reserves a high-gong position in the gong court and sketches a candidate strike language. It does not finalize the gong size, beater, or release geometry.

## Cultural Or Object Language

Breakfast should feel quick, bright, and awake. A small falling ball, ricochet, or rebound strike fits that identity because it reads as light and crisp before the sound happens.

## Input

- Physical input: routed ball, small falling tab, latch release, or cable signal arriving from the field.
- Energy input: preferably a low-energy control signal.
- Trigger condition: breakfast path reaches the gong court or releases local stored energy.

## Output

- Physical output: ball or beater contacts the high gong once.
- Energy output: acoustic strike energy at the gong.
- Signal or route output: breakfast run complete, with a captured striker or ball after impact.

## Energy Source

Candidate sources:

- Local falling ball staged near the high gong.
- Small gravity drop.
- Rebound from a guided ramp.

The system should not need to carry strike energy all the way from the left trigger.

## Reset Or Arming Action

- Return the ball or beater to its starting perch.
- Confirm the catch or rebound pocket is clear.
- Reset the release latch if one is used.

## Materials And Sourcing

- 3D printed: ball ramp, rebound pocket, latch, capture cup.
- Bought: small tuned high gong, steel ball, rubber or felt stops, shoulder screws.
- Scavenged/found: spoon-like striker, small cup, kitchen bell hardware.
- Shop-made: gong bracket, safety catch, adjustable ramp.

## Mounting And Depth

- Approximate footprint: upper gong court, around `66-76 in` from left and `20-31 in` down from top.
- Approximate projection: `2-5 in`.
- Mounting assumptions: gong needs acoustic clearance from the backer and a catch that keeps the ball on-wall after impact.

## Risks

- Force: too little energy may produce a weak sound; too much may double-strike.
- Friction: ball guides may slow the strike.
- Tolerance: small ramp changes may alter sound dramatically.
- Timing: rebound must not interfere with later route elements.
- Safety: ball must be captured after the strike.
- Reset complexity: ball reset should be obvious and reachable.

## Tuning Notes

- Test ball size, drop height, and contact point once candidate gongs exist.
- Compare direct falling strike versus rebound strike for sound and spectacle.
