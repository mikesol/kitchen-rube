# Mechanism: Shared Launch And Diverter

## Status

Dream

## Purpose

Transform the selected meal state from the trigger column into an early routing decision.

This mechanism determines whether the three meals share an initial launch and then diverge, or whether they begin as three independent physical starts that later cross through the same field.

## Cultural Or Object Language

The diverter should make choice visible. It can look like a small railway switch, kitchen scale, turnstile, sorting gate, or decision fork, but the object must actually direct force or motion.

## Input

- Physical input: selector position, released ball, cable pull, falling tab, or launch lever from the trigger column.
- Energy input: human hand energy plus a low-energy rolling or latch signal.
- Trigger condition: a meal state is set before launch.

## Output

- Physical output: routed rolling body, opened gate, released latch, or redirected cable.
- Energy output: low-energy route signal into the entangled field.
- Signal or route output: breakfast, lunch, or dinner path enters the shared mechanism field.

## Energy Source

Human-set selector state, rolling body gravity, small spring, or latch-held gate.

## Reset Or Arming Action

- Restore diverter gate to neutral or selected state.
- Confirm no ball or tab is trapped in an ambiguous position.
- Re-seat any launch body.

## Materials And Sourcing

- 3D printed: switch rails, gate body, detent wheel, ball pockets.
- Bought: bearings, shoulder screws, low-friction tape, small springs.
- Scavenged/found: toy train switch parts, kitchen timer parts, small hinges.
- Shop-made: rail pair, stops, access cover, backer plate.

## Mounting And Depth

- Approximate footprint: near the left-center of the active field, around `12-26 in` from the left and `24-38 in` down from the top.
- Approximate projection: `2-4 in`.
- Mounting assumptions: must remain visible and reachable for reset, with enough clearance for diverter travel.

## Risks

- Force: if a ball is routed, it must not lose so much speed that downstream mechanisms starve.
- Friction: switch points and gates can bind.
- Tolerance: small misalignment may create jams or wrong-route behavior.
- Timing: the route must be set before the launch arrives.
- Safety: moving gates need stops so nothing leaves the wall.
- Reset complexity: a half-reset diverter could send a meal down the wrong route.

## Tuning Notes

- The first proof should compare early split versus shared launch.
- Keep this mechanism reversible until the route identities are stronger.
