# Mechanism: Left Meal Trigger Column

## Status

Sketch

## Purpose

Transform a human meal choice into a clear mechanical start condition for breakfast, lunch, or dinner.

This mechanism owns the left-side input area. It decides how the machine begins, but it should not force the rest of the wall into three separate lanes.

## Cultural Or Object Language

The trigger column is the ritual interface. It should feel like choosing a meal, not pressing an appliance button.

Possible languages:

- Three separate pulls, tabs, handles, or levers.
- One selector plus one launch gesture.
- Kitchen objects used as handles if they still perform real mechanical work.

## Input

- Physical input: hand pull, push, lift, slide, or selector movement.
- Energy input: human hand energy.
- Trigger condition: a meal has been selected and released intentionally.

## Output

- Physical output: release of a ball, cable, latch, gate, or small stored control signal.
- Energy output: low to moderate control energy, not final gong-strike energy.
- Signal or route output: breakfast, lunch, or dinner route identity sent into the shared field.

## Energy Source

Human hand energy, possibly followed by a hand-loaded rolling body, spring latch, or falling tab.

## Reset Or Arming Action

- Return trigger handles or selector to neutral.
- Re-seat any released ball, latch, or tab.
- Confirm the next run has a visible armed state.

## Materials And Sourcing

- 3D printed: selector gates, latch guides, handle brackets, cable fairleads.
- Bought: small bearings, knobs, eyelets, cord, springs, magnets, detents.
- Scavenged/found: kitchen hooks, measuring spoons, drawer pulls, small handles.
- Shop-made: backer strip, lever arms, stop blocks, labels if needed.

## Mounting And Depth

- Approximate footprint: left `8-10 in` of the active field, roughly `10-64 in` down from the top.
- Approximate projection: `1-3 in` for normal controls; avoid anything that catches clothing near the door.
- Mounting assumptions: fixed to a modular backer strip or narrow panel with hidden reinforcement.

## Risks

- Force: triggers must release reliably without needing a hard yank.
- Friction: cable or sliding selectors may bind if mounted slightly crooked.
- Tolerance: selector detents must be obvious and repeatable.
- Timing: simultaneous or partial meal selection could create an ambiguous route.
- Safety: do not interfere with the door, handle, aprons, or normal movement.
- Reset complexity: the user must be able to see whether the column is armed.

## Tuning Notes

- Decide whether the final interface is three triggers or selector plus launch after the shared diverter is better understood.
- Test accidental activation and partial-selection behavior before committing hardware.
