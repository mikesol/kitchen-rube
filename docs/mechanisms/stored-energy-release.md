# Mechanism: Stored Energy Release

## Status

Dream

## Purpose

Capture the common design pattern of local stored energy released by a smaller signal.

This is not one specific mechanism yet. It is a dossier for hanging weights, cocked levers, springs, pendulums, gravity hammers, and latch-held releases that may appear across the field.

## Cultural Or Object Language

Visible stored energy gives the wall latent potential. A cocked lever, suspended weight, or armed pendulum tells the viewer that the machine is waiting.

The excess can be theatrical, but it must still perform work.

## Input

- Physical input: trip tab, routed ball, cable pull, lever bump, or selector state.
- Energy input: low-energy control signal.
- Trigger condition: latch, catch, or gate releases the armed element.

## Output

- Physical output: falling weight, swinging arm, released spring, turning pulley, or opened gate.
- Energy output: amplified local motion or strike energy.
- Signal or route output: downstream release, transfer, delay, or gong strike.

## Energy Source

Hand-armed gravity, spring, counterweight, pendulum, or cocked lever energy.

## Reset Or Arming Action

- Rehang, lift, cock, wind, or latch the stored-energy element.
- Verify the catch is seated.
- Confirm the released path is clear.

## Materials And Sourcing

- 3D printed: latch faces, guards, pulley mounts, stops, indicators.
- Bought: cord, springs, pulleys, weights, bearings, fasteners, anchors.
- Scavenged/found: fishing weights, small tools, clock weights, kitchen weights, drawer slides.
- Shop-made: brackets, catches, tethers, reinforcement plates.

## Mounting And Depth

- Approximate footprint: variable, but likely near gong court and major route transitions.
- Approximate projection: `2-7 in` depending on moving mass and swing path.
- Mounting assumptions: any loaded mechanism needs reliable panel anchoring, stops, and visible arming state.

## Risks

- Force: stored energy can exceed what the wall, part, or gong can tolerate.
- Friction: loaded latches can jam under side load.
- Tolerance: small geometry errors can cause false releases or failures to release.
- Timing: a stored-energy event may feel disconnected if the triggering signal is hidden.
- Safety: moving masses need stops, guards, catches, or tethers.
- Reset complexity: distributed reset is desired, but not if arming becomes ambiguous.

## Tuning Notes

- Start with the lowest energy that performs the job.
- Store strike energy near the final action instead of sending high-energy motion across the wall.
- Every concrete stored-energy mechanism should eventually get its own dossier or experiment.
