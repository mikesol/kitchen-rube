# Mechanism: Dinner Low Gong Strike

## Status

Dream

## Purpose

Transform the dinner route signal into a low-pitched gong strike with heavier visual commitment.

This dossier reserves the low-gong position and identifies the safety implications of a stronger local striker.

## Cultural Or Object Language

Dinner should feel slower, heavier, and more final. A cocked mallet, gravity hammer, or weighted release fits the low gong because the stored energy is visible before it moves.

## Input

- Physical input: latch release, cable pull, falling weight trigger, or routed ball from the field.
- Energy input: low-energy control signal plus locally stored strike energy.
- Trigger condition: dinner path releases the local striker.

## Output

- Physical output: mallet, hammer, or weighted beater contacts the low gong once.
- Energy output: acoustic strike energy at the gong.
- Signal or route output: dinner run complete, with striker stopped or caught safely.

## Energy Source

Local stored energy: gravity hammer, cocked mallet, small hanging weight, spring-assisted striker, or counterweighted arm.

## Reset Or Arming Action

- Re-cock the mallet or raise the gravity hammer.
- Set the release latch.
- Confirm catch, stop, or tether is ready.

## Materials And Sourcing

- 3D printed: latch geometry, safety guard, adjustable stop, trigger coupler.
- Bought: low tuned gong, hinge or pivot hardware, beater head, cord, small weight if needed.
- Scavenged/found: kitchen mallet, small hammer, ladle, weighted utensil.
- Shop-made: reinforced bracket, catch, secondary tether.

## Mounting And Depth

- Approximate footprint: lower gong court above the outlet caution area, around `65-78 in` from left and `47-61 in` down from top.
- Approximate projection: `4-7 in`, potentially theatrical but still controlled.
- Mounting assumptions: needs stronger anchoring than the high and medium strikes, plus a hard stop.

## Risks

- Force: too much stored energy can damage the gong, bracket, or wall.
- Friction: a heavier striker may bind at the latch.
- Tolerance: latch geometry must release predictably under load.
- Timing: slow release can make the route feel unclear if the cause is hidden.
- Safety: stored energy, moving mass, and rebound require physical stops or a tether.
- Reset complexity: arming must not require reaching into a hazardous path.

## Tuning Notes

- Test off-wall before integration.
- Start with low mass and small drop height, then increase only if sound requires it.
