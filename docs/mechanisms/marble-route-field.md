# Mechanism: Marble Route Field

## Status

Dream

## Purpose

Describe the shared rolling-body network that can carry route identity, timing, and visible continuity through the entangled active field.

This is not a commitment that every route uses marbles. It reserves the idea that rolling bodies can be a major language of travel and decision.

## Cultural Or Object Language

Marbles, steel balls, and ball bearings make causality easy to read. They are small, fast, and legible, but the track language should vary by route so the machine does not become one repeated marble run.

## Input

- Physical input: released ball, dropped ball, diverted ball, or ball entering from a trigger/selector.
- Energy input: gravity from height, possibly hand-loaded.
- Trigger condition: ball enters a track, funnel, sorter, switchback, or captured channel.

## Output

- Physical output: ball exits at a known location, trips a latch, transfers momentum, or enters a capture.
- Energy output: rolling kinetic energy, impact impulse, or control signal.
- Signal or route output: visible meal route, timing delay, release trigger, or downstream decision.

## Energy Source

Gravity and hand-loaded starting height. Some route segments may use counterweights or lifts, but those should be treated as separate mechanisms if they become important.

## Reset Or Arming Action

- Return balls to start pockets or route-specific magazines.
- Clear jams and captures.
- Confirm gates and catches are in the intended state.

## Materials And Sourcing

- 3D printed: rails, funnels, switchbacks, splitters, drop tubes, ball captures.
- Bought: steel balls, marbles, bearings, clear tube, aluminum rail, magnets if needed.
- Scavenged/found: toy track, kitchen funnels, wire racks, drawer slides.
- Shop-made: wooden or acrylic rail pairs, stops, adjustable brackets.

## Mounting And Depth

- Approximate footprint: distributed through the entangled field, especially between trigger column and gong court.
- Approximate projection: `1-4 in` for most captured tracks, more for funnels or spiral features.
- Mounting assumptions: tracks should mount to modular backer panels with adjustment slots where possible.

## Risks

- Force: balls may arrive too fast, too slow, or with inconsistent bounce.
- Friction: track material, dust, wall angle, and printed layer texture will matter.
- Tolerance: small alignment changes can cause derailment.
- Timing: route travel time may vary by ball material and starting height.
- Safety: all balls must stay captured or land in deliberate catch zones.
- Reset complexity: loose balls should not become hidden or hard to retrieve.

## Tuning Notes

- Track language should vary by meal route: quick and high for breakfast, balanced and switchable for lunch, slower or heavier for dinner.
- Use experiments to compare rail pairs, troughs, funnels, and captured tubes.
