# Mounting Safety

The installation should feel like it is on the wall, not like a cabinet hung in front of it. Mounting choices serve that feeling and protect the room.

## Principles

- Use modular panels or slats rather than mounting every mechanism directly to the wall.
- Keep the outlet, pipes, door, and normal movement paths clear.
- Put deeper protrusions high or toward the right/back of the kitchen.
- Use strong anchors or studs for panels and any mechanism with stored energy, falling weights, or impact.
- Loaded mechanisms (anything with stored energy or moving mass) need reliable panel anchoring, hard stops, and a visible armed state. The mounting has to make failure obvious before it becomes dangerous.

## Mechanism Class Notes

- **Gongs** — need acoustic clearance from the backer panel. A gong bolted flush to the panel will sound dull and may transmit vibration into the mounting. Plan a small standoff for every gong.
- **Pendulum beaters** — the planned swing arc must be checked against the outlet zone and against any place a person might stand, lean, or reach. The arc envelope, not just the rest position, is what matters.
- **Trigger column controls** — keep projection at 1–3 in. Anything deeper near the door risks catching clothing or aprons during normal kitchen movement.
- **Low-gong striker** — needs stronger anchoring than the high and medium strikes. Test heavy strikers off the wall before integrating them.

## Unknowns To Resolve

These should land here as concrete answers before any heavy mechanism is integrated:

- Stud locations behind the active field.
- Panel material and thickness.
- Anchor type (toggle, expansion, into-stud, etc.) for panels and for individual loaded mechanisms.
- Maximum loaded weight per panel.
- Which specific mechanisms require a secondary safety tether.
