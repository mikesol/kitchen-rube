# Stored Energy Safety

Stored energy is allowed and expected: springs, hanging weights, cocked levers, pendulums, gravity hammers, and latch-held releases. These mechanisms are part of the wall's intended language. The point of this document is to keep that language honest, not to ban it.

## Rules

- Prefer low-mass, low-speed control signals when possible.
- Start with the lowest stored energy that performs the job; escalate only when sound, motion, or reliability genuinely require it.
- Store strike energy near the final action instead of carrying large energy across the wall.
- Add stops, catches, guards, or tethers where a released object could leave its intended path.
- Test stored-energy mechanisms off-wall before integrating them.
- Make every stored-energy element's armed state visible. A weight hidden behind a panel is harder to reset and harder to trust.
- Document every reset or arming action where the mechanism is described (pantry entry, eventual dossier).

## Mechanism Class Cautions

- **Gong strikers** — the low gong needs stronger anchoring than the high and medium strikes. Start any heavy striker with low mass and small drop height, then increase only if the sound demands it.
- **Pendulum beaters** — the swing arc must not cross the outlet zone or a human path. A double-tap blocker or catch is required before a pendulum becomes part of an integrated route.
- **Hanging weights and counterweights** — need reliable panel anchoring, hard stops, and visible armed state. A weight that can swing freely past its catch is the most common stored-energy failure mode.
- **Spring latches** — guard the captive output stroke. A spring that flicks an unguarded tab is fine in a pantry sketch and unacceptable on an integrated wall.

## Open Limits

These need concrete numbers before Phase 4 proof-of-concept work:

- Maximum hanging weight per panel mounting strategy.
- Maximum spring extension or compression for snap-latch elements.
- Maximum allowed protrusion at different wall heights.
- Gong striker safety envelope: peak speed, mass, and rebound containment.
